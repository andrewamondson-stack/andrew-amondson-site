Forest Cathedral

A forest of mirrored mobiles inside a 13th-century tithe barn — Messums West, Wiltshire.

Forest Cathedral is a space for understanding as well as experience. Forest Cathedral transforms the tithe barn at Messums West, with its tree-like beams, into a living forest of light and sound. A kinetic sculpture of branching limbs and mirrored leaves gently moves with air currents created by visitors, filtering light through this forest canopy. Layered shadows animate the floor below while reflected light illuminates the ancient timbers above.

A dimensional soundscape rises from grounding tones of the forest floor and root systems through rustling mid-range frequencies to the bright presence of canopy birdsong. Forest Cathedral unites light and sound as wavelengths that move throughout the space. The Forest Cathedral installation is designed to tour England's most significant Gothic cathedrals; the start of this journey will be at Winchester Cathedral in February 2027.

Walking With

Walking With Our Future Ideas — Festival of Future Nows, Neue Nationalgalerie, Berlin, 2025.

Where do we go from here? Artist Andrew Amondson invited visitors to walk with him and with the ideas that will shape our shared futures with the living world. These generative walks investigate 'openings', or moments when walking together creates the necessary space, time, and attention for creative connection and collective agency. Each walk took twenty-two minutes, followed by a period of shared reflection.

As part of that reflection, each visitor was invited to draw a line on an aerial map of Neue Nationalgalerie, remembering their route around the museum and marking the spots where they felt a connection or resonance, finishing with three short reflections on the back of a postcard. A selection of those postcards, transcribed and translated between English and German, can be seen here.

Walking With Our Future Ideas is a performance of Walking With, a walking-based research and creative practice by Andrew Amondson.

Walking With

Studio Olafur Eliasson, Berlin, 2024–2025.

In spring 2024, Andrew Amondson invited Studio Olafur Eliasson to go for a walk. Motivated by a 2014 Stanford study that found a person's creative output increases by an average of 60% when they are walking, Andrew proposed exploring the effects of integrating walking into the work day as a way to provide space, time, and attention for creative ideation and connection. Andrew's project, titled Walking With, set out to explore the cognitive and embodied potentials of walking as a creative practice.

Framed by EER, Walking With grew at the studio within the Garden, an experimental space at the heart of the studio's working culture, where there are no intended outcomes and where the freedom to explore, speculate, and experiment without the pressure of measurable results is actively cultivated.

Wondering Ways

Wondering Ways is a living art installation, transforming the Grand Ballroom in the Bath Assembly Rooms by filling it with tall grasses to wonder through, to find both shared and private spaces for reflection.

A Wood Henge made from a 250 year old fallen beech tree, the same age as the Assembly Rooms, was re-imagined to honor its history and a hopeful future.

Wondering Ways was co-created with Feilden Clegg Bradley Studios for Forest of Imagination 2023 - Assemble in the Forest, and features music by Cosmo Sheldrake.

Where We Find Ourselves

A permanent art installation in the Playlab of the Museum of Science and Technology Leonardo da Vinci, Milan.

Where We Find Ourselves is an invitation to explore the forest, touch sound, and find yourself within the forest's chorus. It was created at the museum's invitation and focuses on our connection to nature and one another.

A bird sings when it feels safe, and research shows that a bird's song both calms us and helps us self-regulate. When a child feels safe, it creates the space where imagination can grow.

The sounds come from citizen science recordings of birds and other species geolocated in and around Milan, with a score composed by Ananda Costa. The nests and eggs are courtesy of the Andrea Salvetti family.

The Living Tree

2021–2026
Winner of the Inspire Future Generations Prize, 2022

The Living Tree Mirror Maze invited visitors and participants to walk through a geometric maze of mirrors into a living forest of sound, light and sensory experiences. The sound installation by musician Cosmo Sheldrake was composed using nine years of recorded amber-listed British birdsong, creating a beautiful and haunting soundscape for the Living Tree Forest. Designer Matthew Leece created a FUNgal network of connecting and communicating tubes to represent the concept of a mycelium network in the Egg Theatre café, inviting visitors into a deeper understanding of how the forest and humans are all connected, and to learn like a forest (Bateson 2023).

Forest of Imagination

Selected installations and invitations, made as Lead Artist since 2021.

Forest of Imagination is an annual, free arts and architecture event in Bath. Each year it takes a familiar space in the city and reimagines it as a forest, to spark creativity and bring people closer to nature. It is run by House of Imagination with Grant Associates, Feilden Clegg Bradley Studios and Bath Spa University, together with Bath's creative, cultural and educational community.

Over the years it has taken over Queen Square, the spaces around Bath Abbey, the Assembly Rooms and the Holburne Museum and Gardens. Most recently it has taken root at Entry Hill, a forgotten green space the organisers hope will become a permanent Arts and Nature Park.

As Lead Artist since 2021, Andrew Amondson has made the installations and invitations that follow, inviting connection and wonder.

The Water's Way

Entry Hill and the American Museum & Gardens, Bath, 2026.

Hundreds of meters of shimmering blue and white fabric billow and lift on the wind, daylighting the ancient Lyn Brook buried beneath Entry Hill. In urban planning, daylighting is the practice of bringing rivers back to the surface of towns and cities, unburying them from the dark tunnels in which they have been confined, and letting the water meet the sun again. Here, the brook returns not in water but in light and air and moving translucent fabric: a river conjured from memory, flowing once more across the hillside.

The work invites soft fascination; attention held without effort, the kind that doesn't deplete but restores. Let your gaze follow the ribbon of fabric as it lifts and falls. Let it drift. The mind, given permission to wander, begins quietly to recover.

Photo: Emma Seal

The Big Pink Caterpillar

Entry Hill, Bath, 2025.

The Big Pink Caterpillar is a 50-meter-long tunnel sculpture crafted from coppiced willow and repurposed pink ripstop fabric, crawling playfully up the green landscape toward the future site of the Butterfly House designed by Feilden Clegg Bradley Studios. This whimsical land art installation embodies the long-term vision for the Entry Hill site as a destination where art, nature, and transformation converge.

At its heart, The Big Pink Caterpillar is a color experiment that fundamentally shifts visitors’ perception of place. As people journey through the pink-tinted tunnel, their visual adaptation recalibrates. Upon emergence, the greens appear impossibly greener, the natural world more vivid and alive. This phenomenological shift demonstrates how our perception of nature is not fixed but malleable, shaped by experience, context, and wonder.

Collective Daydreaming

Entry Hill, Bath, 2026.

Twelve blue hammocks flow through a grove of trees on Entry Hill, connecting to the River of Dreams, the theme of Forest of Imagination in fall 2026. Collective Daydreaming asks visitors to do one thing: nothing. The hammocks cradle. Sound and light filter down through the leaves.

If something comes from the nothing, there are paper and markers to record it.

The work draws on Andrew Amondson's research into soft fascination, a key part of Attention Restoration Theory. Soft fascination holds our attention gently and lets the mind wander, restoring our depleted directed attention. Nature does this on its own. This drop-in intervention asks how we can design for it, and does something radical in return: it invites us to stop.

Like the other works in this frame, Collective Daydreaming is about creating the conditions for creative thinking, for the imagination to take root.

Walking With · Festival of Future Nows

Postcards from Our Future Ideas

During the Festival of Future Nows at the Neue Nationalgalerie, Walking With Our Future Ideas invited visitors to walk the museum's grounds alongside strangers and talk about the future. After the walk, each person filled in a postcard: a line drawn by hand across an aerial view of the site, and three short answers reflecting on their walk.

Eighteen of those postcards are collected below, front and back, in whichever language the person reached for. Click a card to turn it over and read what they wrote, then switch between the original and its translation.
